**Somewhere in a drawer at your cottage is a receipt for the wrong oil filter.**

Maybe a forum thread said your 115 takes the same filter as your buddy's. Maybe a parts site matched on horsepower and ignored the serial number. Either way, Mercury builds two different 115s with two different filters, and horsepower alone won't tell you which one is on your transom.

Mercury actually publishes all of this in a dealer chart most owners never see. ## Who this is for

Owners of any modern Mercury four-stroke outboard from 2.5 to 600 HP who want the exact factory part numbers for routine maintenance. If you run a two-stroke or a pre-2000s motor, these tables won't cover you; the numbers below apply to the current four-stroke families in Mercury's Feb 2024 reference guide.

## Serial Number First, Horsepower Second

Before you touch the tables, find your serial number. It's on a plate on the transom bracket, and it decides everything.

Mercury has built multiple generations of the same horsepower. A 15 HP before serial 0R833820 is a carbureted motor that takes 10W-30. From that serial on, it's an EFI motor that takes 25W-40. Same badge on the cowl, different oil, different filter, different kit.

The big serial breaks to know:

- **15/20 HP:** carb vs EFI at serial 0R833820
- **75-115 HP:** 1.7L block (1B366823-2B094995) vs 2.1L block (2B095049 and up)
- **Verado 200-300 I6:** maintenance kits change at serial 2B144123
- **V6 and V8 (175-300 HP):** current parts apply from serial 2B529482 up

If you're not sure how to decode yours, our [serial number guide](/blog/how-to-read-mercury-outboard-serial-number) walks through it.

## 100-Hour Kits vs 300-Hour Kits: Which One You Actually Need

Mercury's maintenance kits bundle the parts for the scheduled service in one box, at less than the parts cost individually in most cases.

**The 100-hour kit** covers the annual service: oil filter, fuel filter, gaskets, and drain plug seals. If you boat a normal Rice Lake season (50-100 hours a year), this is your every-year kit.

**The 300-hour kit** adds the bigger-interval items: water pump components, thermostat pieces, and on belt-driven motors, the belt. Think of it as the 100-hour kit plus the wear parts that live on a three-year clock.

The rhythm is simple: 100-hour kit annually, 300-hour kit every third year. Our [maintenance intervals guide](/blog/mercury-maintenance-intervals-20-100-300-rule) breaks down what gets done at each stage and why the hour counter isn't the only trigger. Water pump impellers in particular age out before they wear out; we covered what actually happens to them in our [impeller replacement post](/blog/mercury-impeller-replacement-when-they-fail).

## Oil and Gear Lube: The Spec Details People Get Wrong

**Engine oil.** Mercury four-strokes run either 10W-30 or 25W-40 depending on model, and both must carry the **FC-W** marine four-stroke rating. TC-W3 is two-stroke oil. It's not a "marine oil" catch-all, and it does not go in your four-stroke. Capacities are in the tables above; for a deeper dive on fill volumes, see our [oil capacity chart](/blog/mercury-outboard-oil-capacity-chart).

**Gear lube comes in two grades, and they're not interchangeable spec-for-spec:**

- **Premium Gear Lube (802844K02 / 858058K01):** small motors and standard gearcases up to 60 HP
- **High Performance Gear Lube (858064K01):** Command Thrust gearcases and everything from 75 HP up

If your gear lube comes out looking like coffee with cream, stop and read our [milky gearcase oil post](/blog/milky-gearcase-oil-meaning-cost-ontario) before you just top it up. That colour means water, and water means a seal problem.

## Anodes: Aluminum for Rice Lake

Anodes are the one item on Mercury's chart where the answer depends on your water, not just your motor.

- **Aluminum anodes** work in fresh, brackish, and salt water. They're the right call for Rice Lake and the Kawarthas.
- **Magnesium anodes** are freshwater-only and more active. Fine here, wrong the moment the boat sees salt.

Mid-range motors (40-115 HP) use anode kit 8M0107547. Larger families split by gearcase and trim configuration, and the anode list gets long enough that we'd rather confirm against your serial number than have you order three wrong ones. The rule that matters: replace anodes when they're half gone, and never paint them.

## Common mistakes

- **Ordering by horsepower alone.** The 75-115 family and the Verado I6 both changed parts mid-run. The serial break decides, not the badge.
- **Automotive filters and oil.** Car filters lack the anti-drainback and burst specs for marine duty, and car oil isn't FC-W rated. The few dollars saved aren't worth a warranty argument.
- **Mixing gear lube grades.** Premium in a Command Thrust or V8 gearcase is the wrong spec for the load those gears carry.
- **Skipping the 300-hour items because "it runs fine."** Impellers and thermostats fail by age and heat cycles, not just hours. Running fine is what every motor does right up until it doesn't.
- **Buying anodes for the wrong water.** Magnesium anodes in salt water erode fast. Aluminum covers you everywhere, including here.

## FAQs

### Are these part numbers current for my motor?

They're from Mercury's Quick Reference Guide, Feb 2024 revision (MER-5746), which covers current four-stroke families back through the serial ranges shown in each table. Mercury supersedes part numbers occasionally, and a superseded number will cross-reference to its replacement when ordered. Always confirm against your serial number, especially near a serial break.

### What's the difference between the 100-hour and 300-hour kits?

The 100-hour kit covers the annual service: oil filter, fuel filter, and the gaskets and seals that job needs. The 300-hour kit adds longer-interval wear parts like water pump components and thermostat pieces. Run the 100-hour kit yearly and the 300-hour kit every third year, or sooner if your hours pile up fast.

### Can I use an automotive oil filter on my Mercury outboard?

No. Marine filters are built to different burst-pressure and anti-drainback standards, and outboards live at sustained high RPM in a way car engines don't. The same goes for oil: it needs the FC-W marine four-stroke rating, not just the right viscosity number on the jug.

### Does my Mercury take 10W-30 or 25W-40?

Depends on the model, not preference. Small portables, the 2.1L 75-115 block, the 150, the V6/V8, and the V12 spec 10W-30. The 15/20 EFI, 40-60, the 1.7L 75-115 block, SeaPro models, and most Verados spec 25W-40. The tables above list each one, and your owner's manual is the final word.

### Are Quicksilver parts the same as Mercury Precision Parts?

Both are Mercury Marine brands, made to the same OEM specifications. Quicksilver is Mercury's aftermarket line; Mercury Precision Parts is the dealer-channel branding. Either keeps you inside factory spec, which matters for warranty coverage.

### Where do I find my Mercury serial number?

On a plate or decal on the transom bracket, usually the port side. It starts with a number-letter combination like 2B or 3B on recent motors. Our serial number guide at /blog/how-to-read-mercury-outboard-serial-number shows exactly where to look and how to read it.
